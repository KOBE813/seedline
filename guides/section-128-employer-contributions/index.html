How Employers Contribute to Trump Accounts (Section 128), step by step.

A step-by-step guide to employer Trump Account contributions under IRC Section 128: the $2,500 limit, the written plan, employee notice, certification, payroll and W-2 code TA.

Guide · Updated Oct 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Proposed rules. Much of this is based on proposed regulations REG-101355-26 (Aug. 11, 2026). They aren’t final and may change, though employers may rely on them for plan years before final rules. Not legal or tax advice.

1. Know the limits

Section 128 lets an employer contribute to the Trump Account of an employee or an employee’s dependent, and the employee excludes up to $2,500 a year from income (indexed after 2027). The limit is per employee, so an employee with three children still has one $2,500 exclusion (Notice 2025-68, Q&A I-1, as quoted in the proposed regulations). Section 128 contributions also count toward each account’s $5,000 annual contribution limit.

Contributions can only go to an account whose holder is in the growth period, which ends December 31 of the year they turn 17.

2. Adopt a separate written plan

Under proposed § 1.128-2(b), the plan must spell out:

  • which classes of employees are eligible
  • contribution rules, including amounts and whether salary reduction through a cafeteria plan is allowed
  • how employees designate the account that receives contributions
  • certification, notice, and reporting procedures
  • the plan year
  • how administrative errors are corrected

The employer must actually follow the plan (proposed § 1.128-2(c)).

3. Decide on salary reduction

Employees may add pre-tax money through a section 125 cafeteria plan, but only to a dependent’s account, never their own (proposed § 1.128-2(d)(7); DOL Technical Release 2026-02). Salary reduction counts toward the same $2,500.

4. Tell employees about the plan

All eligible employees must get reasonable notice of the plan’s availability and terms (proposed § 1.128-2(f)). The rules don’t set a specific number of days.

5. Collect sign-ups and certifications

Each employee names the account and certifies, in writing, that the beneficiary is the employee or their dependent, the beneficiary’s date of birth, and that they know of no reason the beneficiary can’t receive a contribution. You can rely on that unless you know it’s wrong, but you can’t rely on it alone to confirm the account is a real Trump Account (proposed § 1.128-2(d)(4)).

6. Check nondiscrimination

The plan can’t favor highly compensated employees. Three tests apply; see our testing guide.

7. Run it through payroll

Contributions are excluded from federal income tax but are still wages for FICA and FUTA (proposed regulations). Report them in Form W-2 box 12, code TA (2026 W-2 instructions), and give each employee a written statement by January 31; the W-2 can satisfy that (proposed § 1.128-2(g)).

FAQ

How much can an employer contribute to a Trump Account tax-free?

Up to $2,500 per employee per year is excluded from the employee’s income under Section 128, indexed for inflation after 2027. The limit is per employee, not per child.

Do employer Trump Account contributions need a written plan?

Yes. Section 128 requires a separate written plan for the exclusive benefit of employees. The proposed regulations list what it must contain.

Are Trump Account contributions subject to FICA?

Yes, under the proposed regulations. The exclusion is for federal income tax only; FICA and FUTA still apply.
