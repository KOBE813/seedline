Nondiscrimination testing, explained.

How the three Section 128 nondiscrimination tests work under the proposed rules: contributions and benefits, eligibility safe harbor, the 55% average benefits test, exclusions, and correction.

Guide · Updated Oct 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Proposed rules. Much of this is based on proposed regulations REG-101355-26 (Aug. 11, 2026). They aren’t final and may change, though employers may rely on them for plan years before final rules. Not legal or tax advice.

Section 128 borrows its nondiscrimination rules from dependent care assistance programs (section 129). The proposed regulations (§ 1.128-3) set out three tests.

Who counts as an HCE

Highly compensated employee (HCE) is defined by cross-reference to section 414(q). In general that’s a more-than-5% owner, or an employee paid over the threshold in the look-back year. For 2026, the threshold is $160,000 of 2025 compensation (Notice 2025-67).

Test 1: Contributions and benefits

Contributions can’t discriminate in favor of HCEs or their dependents. A program that provides benefits on the same terms for all eligible employees satisfies this.

Test 2: Eligibility

The classification of eligible employees must be reasonable, based on objective business criteria, and not favor HCEs. There’s a facts-and-circumstances test and a numerical safe harbor: the plan passes if its ratio percentage (the share of NHCEs eligible compared with the share of HCEs eligible) is at least the employer’s safe harbor percentage, which is 90% reduced by ¾ of a point for each whole point by which the NHCE concentration exceeds 60%.

Test 3: 55% average benefits

Average contributions to NHCEs must be at least 55% of the average for HCEs, across all of the employer’s Trump account contribution programs, measured on the last day of the plan year. For salary-reduction contributions, employees paid under $25,000 may be disregarded.

Who’s left out of tests 2 and 3

Employees who haven’t reached age 21 and completed one year of service, and certain union employees who aren’t in the plan where the benefit was bargained over (proposed § 1.128-3(e)).

The $1,000 pilot match safe harbor

Contributions that match the federal $1,000 pilot deposit (children born 2025–2028), offered on the same terms to all non-excluded employees, are disregarded for tests 1 and 3, but not for test 2.

Correcting a failure

If the 55% test fails, the program can still be treated as passing if, by the Form W-2 deadline, the employer includes the excess in affected HCEs’ income. If every HCE is above the level of (NHCE average ÷ 0.55), each HCE’s excess is the amount above it; otherwise the reduction may be allocated in any reasonable manner. A corrective notice is also required.

FAQ

Who is a highly compensated employee for Trump Account testing?

HCE is defined by reference to IRC 414(q): generally a more-than-5% owner, or someone whose prior-year pay exceeded the threshold. For 2026 determinations the threshold is $160,000 of 2025 pay (IRS Notice 2025-67).

What is the 55% average benefits test?

Average contributions to non-highly compensated employees must be at least 55% of the average contributions to highly compensated employees, measured as of the last day of the plan year.

What happens if a Trump Account plan fails the 55% test?

Under the proposed rules, the plan can still be treated as passing if, by the Form W-2 deadline, the employer includes the excess amounts in the income of the affected highly compensated employees.
